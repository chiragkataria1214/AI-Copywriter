BEST PRACTICES
Intro Dek: Max 2 sentences or 108 characters
CTAs: Action-first, benefit-forward (“Try It Now,” “See All Shades,” “Get the Set”)
Headlines: Clear over clever (great if you can do both)
Conversational, not salesy
Write to one person, not “our customers”
Product-focused, not brand-first (benefit-led)
Seasonal: Use environment-setting intros (“Long days. Warm nights. Light layers.”). Use seasonal language to make evergreen products feel timely (e.g., “Brighten up for summer”).
Include badges in the products that are new or exclusive
Reviews/Quotes: From 2 to 4 lines, 250 characters max
Don’t use emojis
Always include 2 versions of Subject Line and Preheader

OTHER LEARNINGS
Prospect vs. Existing Customer Segments: When requested, we create 2 versions of the same email: for prospects as an introduction to something new. For customers, more focused on main differentiations or limited edition / exclusivity approaches.
Complexion products: Great to have a solution-based positioning (ex. redness, dark circles, etc.)

SUBJECT LINES BEST PRACTICES
Clear Product Mentions
* Subject lines that call out specific products (e.g., “Neutralizer Pencil,” “Gel Liner in Violet”) performed best.
* Preview texts supported this with color or use-case details.
Be specific about the product and shade.
Urgency and Time-Sensitivity Work
* “Coming Tonight at Midnight” and “Get Yours Before It’s Gone” performed well.
* Preview text like “The Wait Is Over” reinforces urgency.
Combine urgency in both subject and preview to double impact.
Short, Direct Preview Texts Win
* Top previews often had 4–6 words, straight to the point.
Avoid: Overloading the preview with too much explanation or detail.
Avoid General or Inspirational Copy
* Lower-performing emails tend to use more abstract or vague copy (e.g., “Start the new year right”).
Tip: Prioritize clarity over cleverness—especially in subject lines.
Clarity on What’s Inside Wins
* Preview texts like “Glow On The Go” and “Your New Everyday Routine Starts Today” speak to lifestyle or routine improvements.
* These emotionally connected, benefit-led lines create a strong click motivation.
Benefit-First Framing Works
* “Say goodbye to dark circles” outperforms vague claims.
Focus on the problem-solution structure in previews and subjects.
